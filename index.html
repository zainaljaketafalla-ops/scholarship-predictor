    Read with care

      This is a prediction from past records, not an official decision. Scholarship offices review your full application, and about 3 in 10 predictions turn out wrong.

      How the predictor works

      71%correct on 220 test students the model had never seen
      0.77AUC: picks the real grantee out of a grantee and non-grantee pair 77% of the time
      1,084student records, half grantees and half not

      What it does

      It is a logistic regression model built in IBM SPSS. Each answer adds to or takes away from your score, based on how often students with that answer were grantees. A score of 50% or more counts as "likely".

      What it uses, and what it can't see

      It uses every detail in the records, including course, major and both parents' ethnic groups. Grades, achievements and the application itself are not in the records, so the model cannot see them.

      Why the percentage is a score, not a chance

      The records were balanced to half grantees and half non-grantees. That makes the score good for comparing students with each other, but higher than the real chance whenever grantees are less common than that.

      Patterns to be aware of

      • Female students in the records were grantees more often, so the model gives them a higher score. This reflects past records, not a rule.
      • None of the 56 new students in the records were grantees, so new students score very low.
      • Every course and major had exactly half grantees in the records, so they change the score only a little.
      • Many ethnic groups have fewer than 20 students in the records. Answers shared by only a few students give unreliable results, and the predictor flags these.
      For researchers: adjust for the real grantee rate

      If you know what share of students really receive a scholarship, enter it to see a corrected chance alongside the score.
